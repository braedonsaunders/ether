Ether 0.2.8 adds opt-in Usenet repair during streaming and budgeted release availability monitoring.

- Add bounded PAR2 reconstruction for confirmed missing or corrupt articles. Repaired bytes are verified before entering the normal segment cache.
- Warm an alternate source as soon as damage is confirmed, adapt repair deadlines to the published playback buffer, and preserve buffered media for compatible HLS/DASH handoffs.
- Keep repair input, memory, concurrency and wait times bounded; stop obsolete recovery after seeks and source closure. Repair Ahead remains disabled unless the owner enables it.
- Add owner controls for release-feed availability monitoring, with separate request, download and health-check budgets and reserved capacity for playback. Monitoring remains opt-in.
- Join cancelled background health work so abandoned checks cannot continue issuing late article fetches or escape their budgets.
- Fix a concurrent archive-layout diagnostic read and prevent equal-timestamp pre-seek telemetry from overriding the new playback position.
- Mark restarted TS packet counters as discontinuous so buffered handoffs do not report transport corruption.
- Strengthen cross-platform tests with deterministic fan-out coordination, virtual rate-limit timing, bounded DASH output and real paced handoffs.

Includes the native macOS app and DMG, Windows desktop installer, Linux packages, multi-architecture container images, and signed automatic-update manifests.

Validation covers pure-Go build/vet/full tests, the UI suite, focused race checks and real FFmpeg buffered-handoff tests. Repair cannot recover missing data without sufficient surviving source and parity; uninterrupted playback on every physical Plex client is not guaranteed.

The macOS app is ad-hoc signed and is not Apple-notarized; macOS may require Open Anyway. The Windows installer is unsigned and may trigger SmartScreen. Update manifests are cryptographically signed separately.

Ether ships no content, providers, or indexers. Owners supply their own services and are responsible for what they access.
